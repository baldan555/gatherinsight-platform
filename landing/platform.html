Start here

The ideas behind it

Six ideas explain almost every screen. Learn them once and the rest of the platform reads naturally.

Project

The container for one client or one study programme: its team, timeline, surveys and reports. Everyone is invited per project, with a role.

Survey

One questionnaire with its respondents. A project holds as many surveys as it needs; each has its own workspace and menus.

Basic and Advanced

Basic is a ready template — NPS, CSAT, CES, custom or voice — shared as a link, QR code or call list. Advanced adds a sampling design, a frame, quotas, field teams and weights.

Channel

How answers arrive: a web link, the surveyor app face to face, a phone call, a voice recording, or a paper form keyed in later. Every channel lands in the same results.

Evidence

Every answer carries its proof: who gave it, where and when, how long it took, what was said on the recording, and a trust score.

Wave

A study repeated every year or two stays one survey with several waves, so questions can be followed and compared over time.

Online study with a sample

AdvancedPersonal or open links

Use it when A study whose numbers must hold up: a defined population, a sample drawn from a frame, a target and a margin of error.

01

Define the population

Write who the study is about and the eligibility rules a respondent must pass; the rules become the screening questions.

  • Population size for the finite-population correction

02

Choose how to select

Seven designs: simple random, systematic, stratified, PPS, cluster, multistage or quota — each with what it needs and what it gives.

  • Probability designs give a margin of error

Upload the frame

The list of units to draw from, checked for empty rows, duplicates and coverage before anything is drawn.

  • Excel or CSV, with an ID column

Size and draw the sample

The sample size comes from confidence, margin of error, design effect and expected response; the draw uses a recorded seed, so it can be repeated.

  • Reserves per stratum for replacements

05

Set the quality rules

Minimum time, attention checks, consistency rules, the trust threshold and whether doubtful responses are held.

  • A hidden honeypot field catches bots

Check who answered

Methodology

The methods behind the numbers

Every number on screen has a method behind it. These are the main ones; each is computed by the platform itself, with defaults, minimum sizes and rounding written down.

Eight ways to choose respondents

Probability

Simple random

When A complete list and no groups that must be guaranteed.

How Every unit has the same chance n/N; a seeded shuffle picks them.

Probability

Systematic

When A list in a meaningful order — by area or size — for implicit spread.

How Every k-th unit from a random start, with a fractional interval N/n.

Probability

Stratified

When Groups that must each be represented, e.g. every region.

How Proportional, equal or custom allocation; a random draw within each stratum.

Probability

PPS

When Units of very different size, such as villages or schools.

How Chance proportional to size; very large units are taken with certainty.

Probability

Cluster

When Travel cost matters more than spread: visit whole areas.

How Draw clusters, then interview a fixed number of units in each.

Probability

Multistage

When No list of households exists, only of areas.

How Up to three stages; households listed in the field, then drawn by the server.

Non-probability

Quota

When Speed matters and the shares of key groups are known.

How Up to three dimensions; a full cell refuses or overflows.

Non-probability

Open link

When Feedback from whoever comes, with no claim to represent a population.

How No frame; caps per channel and the trust score keep it clean.

How big the sample must be

The completes needed follow from the confidence level, the margin of error, the expected proportion and the population size, then grow with the design effect; invitations divide by the expected response and eligibility.

Weights, step by step

A base weight from the inclusion probability; an adjustment so respondents carry the non-respondents of their class; raking to known shares; trimming of extreme weights. The cost of weighting is reported as the effective sample size.

Response rates

Counted per sampled unit, in the conservative AAPOR RR1 style: units never reached count as eligible, partial answers count as non-response.

Which answers to trust

Each self-completed response gets signals — speed, look-alikes, answer patterns, text, its own checks — weighted and capped per group. Field interviews get a separate suspicion score from timing, place and answer patterns.

When a difference is real

Welch t-tests for means, two-proportion z-tests for shares, chi-square for crosstabs, Benjamini–Hochberg when many tests run at once. Changes between waves use each wave's effective sample size.

When fieldwork will finish

A damped-trend model with a weekday pattern is fitted to completed responses per day; the rest of fieldwork is simulated 1,500 times with day-to-day variation, giving a finish date with its band.

AAPOR rates
Standard definitions of response, cooperation, refusal and contact rates; GatherInsight uses the conservative RR1 style.
Back-check
A second contact to confirm an interview really happened.
CES
Customer Effort Score: the share answering 6–7 on a 1–7 ease scale.
Cluster
A group of units, such as a village, drawn as a whole.
CSAT
Customer satisfaction: the share answering 4–5 on a 1–5 scale.
Design effect
How much a design's variance exceeds that of a simple random sample of the same size.
Effective sample size
The simple random sample size that would give the same precision as the weighted sample.
Frame
The list the sample is drawn from.
Hold mode
Doubtful responses wait out of the results until a person approves them.
Inclusion probability
The chance a unit had of being drawn; its inverse is the base weight.
Margin of error
Half the width of the 95% interval around an estimate.
NPS
Net Promoter Score: promoters (9–10) minus detractors (0–6), in points.
OData feed
A live data connection that Power BI, Tableau and Excel read directly.
Panel wave
A wave that interviews the same respondents as the previous one.
PPS
Probability proportional to size: bigger units are more likely to be drawn.
Quota
A target number of completes for a group, such as women aged 25–34.
Raking
Adjusting weights until the sample matches known shares on several variables.
Reserve
Extra drawn units released to replace ineligible ones in the same stratum.
Seed
The starting value of the random draw, recorded so the sample can be repeated.
Straight-lining
Giving the same scale point to every question, a sign of not reading.
Stratum
A group the population is divided into before drawing, such as a region.
Top box
The share choosing the highest points of a scale.
Tracking key
The link that follows a question across waves, even if its wording changes.
Trust score
0–100 for each self-completed response, from the signals it raised.
